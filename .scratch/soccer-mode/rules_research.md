# Soccer rules dossier for scoreboard configuration

Compiled September 15, 2026, for the `feature/soccer-mode` spec checkpoint. Sources cited per row;
"Confidence" reflects how directly the source states the fact for **NCHSAA** varsity/JV soccer.

Primary sources used:
- NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook (current handbook as of today; the "25-26 Handbook Print Version" PDF URL
  given in the brief now 404s — nchsaa.org/handbook/ links only the 2026-27 PDF today):
  `https://www.nchsaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/26-27-Handbook-For-Website.pdf`
  (Section 4.9 Soccer, pp. 97-99; Section 3.3.8 Penalties for an Ejection, p. 60).
- `https://www.nchsaa.org/yellow-card-tracking/` (secondary restatement of 4.9.1(j), simplified).
- `https://www.nchsaa.org/sports/mens-soccer/` and `.../womens-soccer/` (contact/logistics only —
  no rule text found on either page as of today; they link out to the handbook and to
  `https://nfhs.org` for the NFHS rules book, no PDF embedded).
- NFHS rules text is not freely fetchable (nfhs.org gates the rules book itself), so NFHS rule
  numbers and quotes below come from state-association restatements of the same NFHS Soccer Rules
  Book: the MHSAA 2025-26 Soccer Rules Supplement (a supplement to the *2025-26 NFHS Soccer Rules
  Book*) and the NFHS-published *2024-25 Soccer Guide* (a comparative NCAA/NFHS/IFAB table produced
  by NFHS itself, hosted at assets.nfhs.org) and TASO's kicks-from-the-mark page.

**IMPORTANT correction to the brief's premise:** the "2025-26" handbook PDF at the URL given no
longer exists — NCHSAA has already rolled forward to the **2026-27** handbook (today is
September 15, 2026, inside the 2026-27 school year). The 2026-27 handbook is the current,
authoritative primary source and is what this dossier cites. It resolves the 5-vs-10-minute
conference-overtime question explicitly (see Overtime table): **10-minute periods, not 5.**

## Situations that stop the clock

| Value | Source | Quote | Retrieved | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Goal, penalty kick, yellow card, red card, and at referee discretion (injury, time-wasting) | NFHS's own comparative guide (NFHS column, Rule 7) | "Goal, penalty kick, yellow and red cards, and at discretion of referee, (to assess possible injury, time wasting, etc.)" | 2026-09-15 | secondary (NFHS-published) |
| Substitution by the **leading** team in the **last 5 minutes** of the second half stops the clock | NFHS comparative guide (NFHS column, Rule 7) | "Subs during last 5 mins. of 2nd half by leading team" | 2026-09-15 | secondary |
| Same late-game substitution stoppage extends to overtime | MHSAA 2025-26 Supplement (MHSAA's adoption of an NFHS option) | "The clock shall be stopped when a substitute by the team in the lead is beckoned onto the field in the final five minutes of regulation or overtime." | 2026-09-15 | secondary (state adoption of an NFHS-permitted option; confirm NC uses the same option — handbook is silent) |
| Disqualification (red card / send-off) stops the clock and the player must leave the field | NFHS comparative guide | "Caution (player must go out); injury if referee stops clock and beckons on a coach or ... professional onto the field, player must go out including goalkeeper; disqualification" | 2026-09-15 | secondary |
| Injury stoppage: if the referee beckons a coach/health-care provider onto the field, that player must leave regardless of why the clock stopped | NFHS comparative guide / MHSAA supplement 2025-26 rule-change note (3-3-2b(2)) | "Clarifies that an injured player must leave the field if the referee beckons a coach or appropriate health-care provider." | 2026-09-15 | secondary |
| Period ends **on the horn/clock reaching 0:00**, not on a referee whistle, under NFHS timing convention | NFHS comparative guide | "signal sounds or clock shows 0:00. Timer counts last 10 seconds down to zero." | 2026-09-15 | secondary; NCHSAA handbook does not restate this |

## Overtime format by game type — NCHSAA (2026-27 handbook, current)

| Situation | Value | Source | Quote | Retrieved | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conference varsity game, tied at end of regulation | Two complete **10-minute** periods, **not sudden victory** (full periods played out) | NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook §4.9.1(m) | "If a tie score exists at the end of regulation play during each conference varsity game, there will be two complete 10-minute periods (not sudden victory)." | 2026-09-15 | primary |
| Conference varsity, still tied after both 10-minute OT periods | Game **ends in a tie**; no kicks from the mark | NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook §4.9.1(m) | "If the score is still tied at the conclusion of the two 10-minute overtime periods, the game will result in a tie." | 2026-09-15 | primary |
| Regular-season, **non-conference** game, tied at end of regulation | Game **ends in a tie**; no overtime at all | NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook §4.9.1(m)(1) | "All regular season, non-conference games that are tied at the end of regulation will result in a tie." | 2026-09-15 | primary |
| Non-conference **tournament** play and NCHSAA **playoffs**, tied game | Must be resolved by the **NFHS Tie-breaking Procedure** (kicks from the mark); result counts in won-loss record | NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook §4.9.1(m)(2) | "In non-conference tournament play and NCHSAA Playoffs, tied games must be resolved by the NFHS Tie-breaking Procedure, and those decisions count in a team's won-loss record. One goal is added to the winning team's score, and an asterisk may be placed by the team advancing..." | 2026-09-15 | primary |
| Conference **tournament** games | Not addressed as a distinct category in §4.9.1(m); read literally, a conference-tournament game is still a "conference...game" so it falls under the 2×10-minute-periods-then-tie rule, same as conference regular season | NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook §4.9.1(m) (by omission/plain reading) | (see conference-game quote above) | 2026-09-15 | primary text, but the classification for tournament vs. regular-season conference games is **our inference, not spelled out** — flagged below |

This directly answers the brief's open question: **the current (2026-27) NCHSAA handbook uses
10-minute overtime periods for conference games, not 5.** The "5-minute" figure the brief
attributes to a referee-association summary does not appear anywhere in the current handbook text;
it may be a confusion with the *NFHS pre-overtime interval* (5 minutes before the first OT period,
not the period length itself — see the Halftime/overtime-intervals table above), or a stale figure
from an older handbook edition we did not have direct access to (the 2023-24 text cited in
`CONTEXT_FOR_AGENTS.md` was not independently fetchable today — see Confirm with AD).

## Kicks from the mark procedure

| Value | Source | Quote | Retrieved | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coin toss: visiting team calls; procedure per NFHS Rule 5-2-2d referenced by MHSAA | MHSAA 2025-26 Supplement | "A coin toss shall be held as in Rule 5-2-2d." / TASO: "The referee conducts a coin toss with the visiting team's captain calling the coin. The team that wins the toss decides whether to take the first or second kick." | 2026-09-15 | secondary (state restatements of NFHS procedure) |
| Five kickers per team, chosen by the coach, may include the goalkeeper, from any eligible (non-disqualified) roster player on or off the field | MHSAA 2025-26 Supplement | "Each coach will select any five players, including the goalkeeper, on or off the field (except those who may have been disqualified) to take the kicks." | 2026-09-15 | secondary |
| Kicks alternate between teams; no follow-up/rebound on a kick; defending team may change goalkeeper before each kick | MHSAA 2025-26 Supplement | "Teams will alternate kickers. There is no follow-up on the kick. The defending team may change the goalkeeper prior to each penalty kick." | 2026-09-15 | secondary |
| After 5 kicks each, most goals wins; if still tied, each coach picks 5 **different** players for sudden-victory rounds (score more than opponent with equal kicks taken = win); repeat with new sets of 5 if still tied | MHSAA 2025-26 Supplement | "If the score still remains tied ... continue the sudden-victory kicks with the coach selecting any five players (except disqualified players) to take the next set of alternating kicks." | 2026-09-15 | secondary |
| Scoreboard recording of the result: **add one goal to the winning team's score**; an asterisk may mark that advancement came via tiebreaker | NCHSAA 2026-27 Handbook §4.9.1(m)(2) (matches MHSAA wording) | "One goal is added to the winning team's score, and an asterisk may be placed by the team advancing to indicate advancement was the result of the progression format." | 2026-09-15 | primary (this exact "+1 goal" convention is stated by NCHSAA itself, not just MHSAA) |

## Confirm with the AD

These values could **not** be verified from an NCHSAA-specific primary source today. Each row gives
our recommended default (already reflected in the Setup-drawer table above) plus what we'd want the
AD (or an NCHSAA rules interpreter) to confirm.

1. **Is the scoreboard clock actually the "official" clock for NCHSAA games, or does the referee's
   watch/whistle govern regardless of the display?** No NCHSAA-specific text found; NFHS convention
   (via MHSAA's restatement) says the home-school timer/scoreboard is official. **Recommended
   default: treat the scoreboard as the operator's best-effort display, but never let the app claim
   authority over the official time** — keep a visible note in the UI that the referee's decision
   governs disputes.
2. **Has North Carolina (as a state association) adopted the NFHS option to stop the clock for a
   leading team's late substitution, and does that extend into NCHSAA overtime periods?** The NCHSAA
   handbook's soccer section (§4.9) never mentions this rule at all, positively or negatively.
   MHSAA (a different state) has adopted it; NFHS makes it optional per state. **Recommended
   default: implement the "stop clock for leading-team sub in final 5:00" behavior as an on/off
   Setup-drawer toggle, defaulted ON** (matches NFHS national default; safer to model a real event
   the operator might see, than to silently ignore it), but flag it for the AD to confirm North
   Carolina uses it.
3. **How exactly does a "conference tournament" game get classified** — as a conference game (2×10
   min OT, tie-if-still-tied) or as tournament play (kicks from the mark)? The handbook's overtime
   subsection (§4.9.1(m)) only names "conference varsity game," "non-conference" regular season, and
   "non-conference tournament play and NCHSAA Playoffs" as its three buckets — a **conference**
   tournament isn't named as its own bucket. **Recommended default: treat conference-tournament
   games the same as conference regular-season games (2×10-minute OT, tie stands)**, since they are
   still, textually, "conference...game[s]," but this is our inference and should be confirmed with
   the AD/NCHSAA before locking behavior for conference tournament play.
4. **Whether the current handbook's 10-minute conference-overtime figure is actually a change from
   an older rule, or whether the referee-association summary the brief mentions was simply wrong.**
   We could not independently open a 2023-24 handbook PDF today to compare wording (only 2026-27 was
   linked live from nchsaa.org); CONTEXT_FOR_AGENTS.md's own note cites the 2023-24 handbook as also
   saying two 10-minute periods, so the discrepancy may be entirely in the "ncfsra.com summary" or
   the "referee-association summary," not in the handbook text itself across years. **Recommended
   default: trust the 10-minute figure; if the AD has a specific referee-association bulletin citing
   5 minutes, ask them to send it so we can identify what game type it was describing** (it may be
   describing something else, like the pre-overtime interval, not the period length).
5. **Exact varsity half length (40 minutes) and quarter option (4×20) for NCHSAA specifically** — the
   NCHSAA handbook's soccer section never restates game length at all; it is silent and simply
   defers to the NFHS Soccer Rules Book. We treated the NFHS default (2×40 or 4×20) as NC's rule by
   default, which is standard practice, but it is not independently NCHSAA-stated text.
6. **Whether NC uses halftime = 10 minutes, or some other locally-agreed number** — same gap as #5;
   NCHSAA handbook doesn't restate the NFHS default number.
7. **Whether NCHSAA has adopted the NFHS 2026-27 card-distribution-process change (new Rule
   5-3-1 f-j, officials physically holding cards up)** in a way that affects scoreboard operator
   workflow (e.g., timing of when a card event should be logged) — not confirmed against an
   NCHSAA-specific bulletin, only against the national NFHS rule-change list.
8. **Mercy rule interaction with overtime**: the Nine-Goal Rule text (§4.9.1(k)) says "at any time
   in the second half," which by plain reading would not apply during an overtime period (which is
   not "the second half"). We assumed the rule does **not** re-trigger during overtime — confirm
   with the AD, since a 9-goal overtime blowout would be an extremely unusual edge case but the
   Setup drawer should still have a defined behavior for it.
